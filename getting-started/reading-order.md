# Reading order

You can read the book in two orders.

## 1 · Numerical order

01 to 40. Each lesson assumes only the lessons before it. Prerequisites are listed in every lesson's metadata. This is the order if you want to work through the book cover to cover.

## 2 · Curiosity order (the order the videos are published in)

The Koddan Kasa videos are not published in numerical order but in an order that shows an example from each phase early. Each one stands on its own:

| Order | Lesson | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [02 · Why did the cube go through the table?](../phase-0-world-frame-by-frame/02-timestep.md) | No hardware; a strong twist; shows how simulation works |
| 2 | [01 · The world is a text file](../phase-0-world-frame-by-frame/01-world-as-text.md) | Opens the file the physics engine reads |
| 3 | [04 · Does a heavier cube slip later?](../phase-0-world-frame-by-frame/04-friction.md) | A surprise you can check with high-school physics |
| 4 | [06 · 6 motors, but not 6 degrees of freedom](../phase-1-space-and-body/06-degrees-of-freedom.md) | The real SO-101 appears for the first time |
| 5 | [11 · 12 joints, two numbers](../phase-2-muscle/11-pd-control.md) | The easiest control concept to convey |
| 6 | [19 · Replay is not learning](../phase-4-learning-from-demonstration/19-replay.md) | The difference between memorizing and learning |
| 7 | [24 · The average of two right answers is wrong](../phase-4-learning-from-demonstration/24-two-right-answers.md) | Why plain imitation isn't enough |
| 8 | [31 · Getting a robot to work by talking](../phase-5-agents/31-strands-agent.md) | The showcase of the agents phase |

After these eight, returning to numerical order is easiest: you fill in the gaps.
